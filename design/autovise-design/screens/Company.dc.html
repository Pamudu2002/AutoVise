01 · Investment thesis

Earnings growth, not a re-rating, carries this stock to LKR 318.10.

We see LKR 318.10 as fair value for Lanka Harbour Logistics in three years — 29.6% above today's LKR 245.50. The company is turning rising port volumes into profit faster than its peers, and its balance sheet can fund the next phase of warehouse expansion without new equity.

The market already pays 13.5× earnings, above the sector's 11.8×. We assume that premium narrows, not widens — so the upside comes from what LHL earns, not from what investors are willing to pay for it.

02 · Key drivers
+14% Container volumes

Throughput rose to 1.92 mn TEU in 2025, a record for the group.

31% Higher-margin mix

Contract logistics and warehousing now make up 31% of revenue, up from 24% in 2023.

0.42× Deleveraging

Debt/equity fell from 0.62 in 2021 to 0.42 at June 2026, cutting finance costs.

03 · Valuation walkthrough

We start from 2025 earnings per share of LKR 18.20 and grow them at 11.0% a year — above the sector median of 8.4%, below LHL's own 14.7% four-year record — to reach LKR 24.89 by 2028.

We then apply an exit P/E of 12.0×, a step down from today's 13.5× toward the sector benchmark. That gives LKR 298.69. A +6.5% qualitative adjustment for management quality and contracted revenue takes the base case to LKR 318.10.

04 · Risks
  • Customer concentration. The top three shipping lines account for 46% of throughput.
  • Currency. 38% of operating costs are USD-linked; a weaker rupee squeezes margins.
  • Concession renewal. The terminal operating licence comes up for renewal in 2031, just beyond our horizon.
05 · Catalysts
  • Q3 2026 results, Nov 2026. First full quarter from the new Kelaniya warehouse.
  • Dividend policy review. The board will revisit payouts before the 2027 AGM.
06 · What would change our view
Toward the bull case · LKR 371.80

Throughput growth above 18% for two quarters in a row, or a new long-term contract with a top-five shipping line.

Toward the bear case · LKR 212.40

Losing one of the three largest shipping-line customers, or net margin falling below 8% for a full year.
