How AutoVise builds a prediction

Every target on AutoVise can be traced back to a page in a company's own reports. This page explains each step, the limits of the model, and how you should — and should not — use what it produces.

1. What we predict

For each listed company we publish a share-price range three years ahead: a bear case, a base case and a bull case. The base case is our central estimate; the bear and bull cases show what happens if growth or valuation turn out materially worse or better. We also publish a checkpoint every 12 months so the prediction can be scored as it goes — see the Track Record.

2. Where the numbers come from

Our analysts upload each company's annual and interim reports. The system extracts the financial statements and the management commentary, and every extracted figure carries a page reference. An analyst reviews and approves the extraction before it can affect a prediction. If a report is replaced — for example a restated annual report — the old version is kept, marked as superseded, and the prediction is recalculated.

Share prices are refreshed each trading day. If a company's price or reports go out of date, its card is marked Outdated data and its confidence falls.

3. The valuation model

The base-case target combines three parts:

Target = EPSyear 3 × Exit P/E × (1 + Qualitative tilt) EPSyear 3 = latest full-year EPS × (1 + growth)3
  • Earnings growth. Based on the company's own multi-year record, management guidance in the reports, and the sector median. Capped at 30% a year.
  • Exit multiple. The P/E we expect investors to pay in year three. It starts from today's multiple and moves toward the sector benchmark unless the reports give a clear reason it should not. Capped at 25×.
  • Qualitative tilt. A small adjustment — never more than ±10% — for strengths and weaknesses that do not show up in the numbers yet.

The waterfall on each company's How We Got Here tab shows exactly how much each part adds or subtracts.

4. Qualitative factors

We score six factors from 0 to 100 using the text of the reports: management, growth outlook, competitive position, risk profile, governance and sentiment. Each score comes with a one-line justification and the passages it was drawn from. The six scores are combined into the qualitative tilt; a company with average scores receives no tilt at all.

5. Confidence and the AutoVise Score

Confidence (0–100) tells you how much to trust the prediction itself. It rises with recent, complete, reviewed data and falls when the bear and bull cases are far apart. Below 60 we show it in amber.

The AutoVise Score (0–100) summarises the opportunity: 40% upside to the base case, 40% qualitative quality, 20% confidence. It is a way to sort companies, not a rating to buy.

6. When predictions change

A prediction is recalculated whenever a new report is approved, a report is replaced or deleted, or our analysts change an assumption. The new draft is compared with the published version, and a reviewer must approve it before it goes live. Any manual override is recorded with a note.

7. What the model cannot see

The model only knows what is in the reports and the price history. It does not anticipate takeovers, sudden regulatory changes, fraud or market-wide shocks. It is weakest for companies with short histories, volatile earnings, or reports that are late. Treat every target as one informed view, not a forecast you can rely on.
